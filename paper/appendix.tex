\noindent This appendix collects supplementary results for the main
paper. The main paper's preferred causal estimate is the
block-level binding-state decomposition: a 1 percentage-point
municipal resident-rate increase in the Inc-bind cell reduces the
log count of workers living in the block by roughly $-0.21$ log
points, with a clean realized-dose pre-trend and a null Inc-nobind
within-design placebo. Municipality-level results are used for
policy-jurisdiction magnitude calibration and for the industry
mechanism; the municipality-level Inc-bind coefficient is larger
but the realized-dose pre-trend fails parallel trends, so the
municipality-level estimate is not the causal headline.
School-district-level results are reported as aggregation
diagnostics and are not load-bearing for the main causal claim;
they do not robustly detect a sorting response. Block-level share
outcomes are reported as supporting estimates and are fragile at
block geography.

\noindent Section, table, and figure numbering in this appendix is
self-contained.

\paragraph{Inc-bind: the binding-cell effect.}
A 1 percentage-point increase in the muni resident rate in the Inc-bind
cell (where the bill rises by the full $\Delta\tau$ for in-muni residents)
reduces \texttt{live\_and\_work} by roughly 33 percent (a $-0.40$
log-point coefficient), \texttt{in\_commuters} by roughly 19 percent
($-0.21$ log points), \texttt{total\_jobs} by roughly 24 percent
($-0.27$ log points), and \texttt{total\_residents} by roughly 10
percent ($-0.10$ log points). The dependent variable mean
on \texttt{log(live\_and\_work)} is 3.68, so the typical muni has
$e^{3.68} \approx 40$ live-and-work workers; a 1 pp hike at this mean
predicts $40 \cdot (e^{-0.402} - 1) \approx -13$ live-and-work workers.
Actual rate hikes in the Inc-bind cell average 0.27 pp, which scales
the predicted effect down to roughly $0.27 \cdot 40 \cdot (e^{-0.402} - 1)
\approx -3.6$ workers per typical event over 5 years.

\paragraph{Inc-becomes: the bill-flip cell.}
Inc-becomes events show smaller but still significant effects: $-0.181$
on \texttt{live\_and\_work}, $-0.212$ on \texttt{total\_jobs}, $-0.068$
on \texttt{total\_residents}. The attenuation relative to Inc-bind is
consistent with the bill rising by less than the full $\Delta\tau$ in
this cell---only the portion of the rate change above the work nonresident
rate is incident on the worker.

completeness but are not robust to specification. Dec-bind has 11 munis;
Dec-becomes-non has 4; Dec-nobind has 15. We do not draw asymmetric-response

\section{Data Construction Pipeline (Step-by-Step)}
\label{sec:app-pipeline}

This appendix expands the build pipeline summarized in the main paper's
Data section. The analysis links two administrative systems that do
not share common identifiers: LEHD/LODES employment records (Census
block geography) and Pennsylvania EIT rates (DCED municipality IDs).
The build proceeds through the following steps.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scrape PA DCED MunStats tax rates.}
    PA does not publish a downloadable panel of historical EIT rates.
    The only source is the DCED MunStats portal, which serves one XLS
    file ($\sim$12 MB) per year via an ASP.NET form. A Python script
    submits requests for each year 2000--2026, extracting resident and
    nonresident EIT rates, school district EIT rates, and Local
    Services Tax for all $\sim$2{,}500 municipalities. About 27\% of
    munis report multiple school-district rates; in the municipality-
    level panel each municipality-year is assigned the maximum SD rate.

    \item \textbf{Scrape PA DCED Annual Financial Reports.}
    Fiscal controls (share-based) are constructed from the DCED AFR
    (71 columns per municipality-year) for 2002--2023, trimmed at load
    to 2002--2019: shares of police, streets, debt service in total
    expenditures and shares of EIT, real estate tax, intergovernmental
    revenue in total revenues.

    \item \textbf{Backfill nonresident rates.}
    The DCED nonresident registry was incomplete pre-Act 32: 55--60\%
    coverage 2002--2011, 72\% in 2012, 77\% by 2019. Diagnostics show
    this is a reporting gap, not a regime gap (0.2\% of late-reporting
    munis have a true zero nonresident rate; 96.3\% of nonresident
    rates are perfectly stable across 2012--2019). I backfill missing
    pre-2012 nonresident rates with the earliest observed post-2012
    rate per muni and flag the imputation.

    \item \textbf{Download LODES block-level OD files.}
    LODES Version 8 (2020 Census geography retroactively applied to
    2002--2021), block-level (\texttt{agg\_geo = "block"} in the
    \texttt{lehdr} R package). I download main files (intra-state) and
    auxiliary files (cross-state). All-jobs (S000), earnings (SE01,
    SE03), and age (SA01--SA03) segments. Reference date: April 1.

    \item \textbf{Crosswalk PA DCED to Census geography.}
    DCED municipality names matched to TIGER/Line COUSUBFP via
    normalized name $+$ county; 99.6\% match (9 unmatched munis
    dissolved or merged before 2020).

    \item \textbf{Build a block-to-municipality crosswalk.}
    Point-in-polygon assignment of TIGER 2020 block centroids to PA
    cousub geometries. Because 2020 Census blocks nest in cousubs by
    construction, this assignment is exact: every block has an
    unambiguous municipality. The crosswalk recovers all 2{,}561 PA
    municipalities. The alternative (tract-centroid assignment)
    silently drops 1{,}082 of 2{,}561 municipalities (42\%) and
    misattributes employment for the retained ones.

    \item \textbf{Compute neighbor pairs.}
    Centroid-to-centroid distances between all PA muni pairs; retain
    pairs within 80 km.

    \item \textbf{Aggregate LODES.}
    Block-pair-year LODES is summed to (work~municipality, home~
    municipality)~$\times$~year, producing total jobs, live-and-work,
    in-commuters, and cross-state commuters per municipality-year.
    Block-level outcomes (\texttt{home\_count},
    \texttt{work\_count}, \texttt{home\_in\_muni\_share}) are
    retained directly on the block-year panel without aggregation.

    \item \textbf{Merge rates and controls.}
    Final join: rate panels and fiscal controls onto the OD panels,
    producing the analysis-ready municipality $\times$ year, school-
    district $\times$ year, and block $\times$ year datasets.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Pooled Inc/Dec Binary-Event Analog}
\label{sec:app-pooled-binary}

The binary-event analog of the per-pp pooled specification
(Equation~\ref{eq:pooled}) replaces each $\Delta\tau^s_{it}$ dose
